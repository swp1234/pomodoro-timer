About the Pomodoro Technique: Science, Ultradian Cycles & FAQ

The Pomodoro Technique is an evidence-backed time-management method created by Francesco Cirillo. It organizes focused effort into structured 25-minute work sprints separated by 5-minute cognitive rest intervals.

Productivity Note: This timer is an educational tool designed to support cognitive pacing and sustainable focus. Adapt session intervals to fit your personal workflow and energy levels.

Core Principles of Structured Focus

  • 25-Minute Work Sprint: Singular focus on a single task without multitasking or notification interruptions.
  • 5-Minute Brain Rest: Step away from screens, stretch, and allow dopamine reserves in the prefrontal cortex to replenish.
  • 4 Cycles & Long Break: After 4 consecutive pomodoros, take a 15–30 minute break to reset executive attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Why is 25 minutes the standard Pomodoro length?

A: 25 minutes is brief enough to resist procrastination and distractions while providing enough sustained time to reach a state of productive flow.

Q: How do ultradian rhythms relate to periodic breaks?

A: Nathaniel Kleitman's Basic Rest-Activity Cycle (BRAC) shows human energy naturally fluctuates. Scheduled micro-breaks align with these neurological peaks and valleys.

Q: Can I adjust interval times in settings?

A: Yes. Use the Settings icon to configure custom work durations, short break times, and long break intervals according to your study or work demands.

Q: Is my productivity data saved privately?

A: Always. All session counts, daily streaks, and custom settings remain 100% on your device in local storage. No tracking data is sent to external servers.
